## 1. Jobs to be Done

**Segment:** one person. Saves compulsively on Instagram, revisits almost never. Opens Reel Vault in the evening, phone in bed or laptop, in a relaxed retrieval mood.

**Situation:** "When I half-remember something I saw months ago and want it back…"

**Current alternatives being 'hired':** Instagram's own Saved tab, scrolling the DM thread by hand, giving up.

### Functional jobs

- **[EVIDENCED]** Find one specific reel from a three-year, 198-item hoard using only a half-memory of it — no title, no date, no author. *(53% of items have no usable title; span is 2023-05 → 2026-06.)*
- **[EVIDENCED]** Re-read a DM thread and recover the reels traded inside it. *(192 messages, 189 DM-shared items, one conversation.)*
- **[ASSUMED]** Decide whether something is worth keeping at all, rather than hoarding by default.

### Social jobs

- **[ASSUMED]** Be the person who can actually produce the thing they mentioned — "hold on, I saved that" and then *find it* — rather than the person who says it and never delivers.

### Emotional jobs

- **[ASSUMED]** Feel that saving something means keeping it, not dropping it into a hole. The act of saving should stop feeling futile.
- **[ASSUMED]** Avoid re-entering Instagram to retrieve something, because opening the app costs forty minutes to the feed.
- **[EVIDENCED as design intent]** Feel calm while browsing — retrieval, not a dopamine loop. *(`PRODUCT.md:29`.)*

> The emotional job is likely the real one. Nobody builds a private local archive for a functional reason; they build it because Instagram's Saved tab makes them feel like their own memory is someone else's property.

### Pains

**Challenges**
- **[EVIDENCED]** Nothing in the vault is described. 0/198 summaries, 0/198 notes, 53% untitled — so there is nothing to search *on*. The vault has the content but not the metadata that makes content findable.
- **[EVIDENCED]** Two categories exist and both restate `origin`, so filtering narrows nothing.
- **[EVIDENCED]** The vault is 85 days stale and does not say so anywhere in the UI.

**Costliness**
- **[EVIDENCED]** Enriching the existing 198 items requires 10 manual button presses, behind a button that only appears after a completed sync.
- **[EVIDENCED]** Retrieval currently costs a linear scan of 198 thumbnails, because no filter or search meaningfully narrows.

**Common mistakes**
- **[ASSUMED]** Saving the same reel twice from different surfaces. *(Mitigated: items are unique by URL.)*
- **[EVIDENCED]** Assuming enrichment ran. Nothing in the UI reports that 0% of the vault is enriched.

**Unresolved problems**
- **[EVIDENCED]** Instagram's own Saved tab has the same core defect — no search over content, no notes, no summaries. **Reel Vault currently reproduces that exact defect**, plus a sync step. On today's data it is not yet better than the thing it replaces.

### Gains

**Expectations**
- Describe every item automatically on arrival, with no button to find and no step to remember.
- Answer "the one about the guy explaining kerning" without exact words.

**Savings**
- Retrieval in seconds instead of a full scroll of the hoard, and without opening Instagram.

**Adoption factors** *(adoption = you keep using it instead of abandoning it)*
- It must be **better than the Saved tab on day one**, not after a manual backfill.
- It must be honest about staleness and coverage.

**Life improvement**
- Saving stops being a discard gesture. The hoard becomes a library.

## 2. Problem Statement

**I am:** the sole owner of a three-year Instagram hoard.
- I have saved 198 things and revisited approximately none of them.
- 95% of what I kept arrived through one DM conversation, not the Saved tab.
- I remember *what a thing was about*, never what it was called.

**Trying to:** get back one specific reel out of three years of accumulation, quickly, without opening Instagram.

**But:**
- Nothing I saved is described — no summaries, no tags, no notes, and half of it has no real title.
- Search and chat rank over exactly those empty fields, so they return noise.
- The two filters I have both restate where an item came from, which I already know.

**Because:** the archive was built to *collect* content and treat description as an optional post-processing step — one buried behind a button that only appears after a sync, and that has never run.

**Which makes me feel:** the same futility the Instagram Saved tab produces. I have moved the hoard, not solved it.

### Context & constraints

- Single user, single machine, local SQLite, no scale requirements.
- Sync is deliberately throttled (20h minimum, 6h cooldown) because the private API risks the account.
- Reels are video; the thumbnail is often the only extractable signal without the sidecar.
- 100% of items have a thumbnail — the richest under-used asset in the product.

### Final problem statement

> **Reel Vault has successfully captured three years of saved content and has made none of it findable — because description is treated as an optional step the user must trigger, rather than a property of every item at the moment it arrives.**

## 3. Positioning

### Value proposition

**For** a compulsive Instagram saver who never revisits what they save,
**that need** to actually retrieve one specific thing months later from a half-memory,
**Reel Vault** **is a** private, local archive with automatic recall
**that** describes everything it captures on arrival, so the hoard becomes searchable by what things are *about* — not by what they were called.

### Differentiation

**Unlike** the Instagram Saved tab — and unlike Reel Vault as it stands today —
**Reel Vault provides** a searchable description of every item without the user asking, on a corpus that includes the DM threads Instagram will not let you search at all.

> The differentiation sentence is currently **aspirational, not true.** Today the honest version reads: "Unlike the Saved tab, Reel Vault provides the same unsearchable grid, in a nicer typeface, with a sync step." Closing that gap is the entire point of the redesign.

## 4. Opportunity Solution Tree

### Desired outcome

**Successful retrieval rate: from an assumed near-0% to ≥80%** — of ten deliberate "find the thing I'm thinking of" attempts, eight succeed inside 30 seconds without opening Instagram.

**Why this metric:** it is the only outcome the product exists for, it is measurable by one person in one sitting, and every finding in the audit is a reason it is currently near zero. Vanity metrics (items stored, syncs run) are already high and tell us nothing.

**Leading indicator:** *enriched coverage* — % of items with a real summary. Currently **0%**. Retrieval cannot exceed what coverage permits.

---

### Opportunity 1 — Nothing in the vault is described *(root cause; blocks everything)*

**Problem:** 0/198 summaries, 0/198 notes, 53% generic titles. There is no text to retrieve on.
**Evidence:** audit §2.2, §2.3.

**Solutions**
1. **Enrichment as an ingest step, not a button.** Every item is described as it lands; the manual trigger disappears. *Hypothesis: coverage goes 0% → ~100% and stays there without user action.*
2. **Backfill worker for the existing 198.** A resumable loop rather than 10 manual presses. *Hypothesis: the current corpus reaches full coverage in one unattended run.*
3. **Vision-based description from the thumbnail.** 100% of items have an image and reels are video the text pipeline cannot read; the thumbnail is the only universally available signal. *Hypothesis: usable descriptions even when the sidecar is down and the title is generic.*

---

### Opportunity 2 — The privacy promise is not true

**Problem:** private DM content is posted to Groq while the product sells "local first, private, yours".
**Evidence:** audit §2.5.

**Solutions**
1. **Local-only enrichment** via Ollama, with the cloud path removed. *Hypothesis: the claim becomes literally true; quality cost unknown.*
2. **Disclose and make it a choice** — a one-time consent screen naming the provider and what leaves the machine, defaulting to off.
3. **Local by default, cloud opt-in per batch** — combines 1 and 2; local is the floor, cloud is a deliberate upgrade.

---

### Opportunity 3 — The IA points at the wrong content

**Problem:** the home route is the saved-posts grid (4.5% of the corpus); 95% sits behind the second nav item as a single thread. Category filters restate `origin`.
**Evidence:** audit §2.1, §2.4.

**Solutions**
1. **One unified archive as home.** Drop the saved/DM split as the primary axis; it is a provenance detail, not a retrieval axis. Filter by *what things are about*, once summaries exist.
2. **Make search the home page.** Given the job is retrieval, open on the search field rather than a grid to scroll.
3. **Thumbnail-first browsing with real facets** — date, author, thread, topic — replacing the two decorative categories.

---

### Recommended POC — 1.2, backfill the existing 198

**Why this one:** highest total, and it is the only solution that produces *evidence* rather than more architecture. Nothing else can be judged until the vault has descriptions in it: solutions 3.1–3.3 are all reorganisations of content we cannot yet describe, and 1.1 is worth building only once 1.2 proves the descriptions are good enough to retrieve on.

**Hypothesis:** *If every one of the 198 items carries a real summary and tags, then successful retrieval on ten deliberate searches rises from near-0 to ≥80%, because search will finally have content to match against.*

**Experiment — concierge, one sitting, no new UI:**
1. Run the backfill over all 198 items.
2. Manually inspect a random 20 summaries. **Gate: ≥16/20 must be recognisably about the right reel.** If this fails, the problem is enrichment *quality* (→ 1.3, vision) and the IA work must wait.
3. Write down ten things you actually want to find, *before* searching.
4. Search for each. Record hit/miss and seconds-to-find.

**Success:** ≥8/10 found in ≤30s.
**If it fails:** the miss transcript tells you whether the gap is coverage, description quality, or ranking — and that decides the next branch. That transcript is worth more than the rest of this document.

**Deliberately not first:** 2.1/2.3 (the privacy fix is severity-critical but blocks nothing — do it *alongside*, via 2.2's disclosure, before any cloud backfill runs), 3.x (reorganising undescribed content is rearranging the hoard).

## 5. Roadmap

### Now — make the vault describable *(the whole bet)*

| Initiative | Outcome | Metric |
|---|---|---|
| Backfill worker over the existing 198 | Corpus becomes searchable at all | Coverage 0% → 100% |
| Disclosure before any cloud enrichment (2.2) | Privacy claim becomes honest | Consent shown before first egress |
| Retrieval experiment (10 searches, recorded) | We learn whether this product works | ≥8/10 found in ≤30s |
| Fix `CLAUDE.md` / `PRODUCT.md` drift; drop `@anthropic-ai/sdk` | Docs match the system | — |

**Everything in Now is gated on the retrieval experiment. Do not start Next before it runs.**

### Next — make description automatic and honest

| Initiative | Outcome | Metric |
|---|---|---|
| Enrichment at ingest (1.1) | Coverage stays at 100% without user action | New items enriched within one sync |
| Coverage + staleness surfaced in the UI | User can trust what they are looking at | "85 days stale, 100% described" visible |
| Vision description from thumbnail (1.3) *if the quality gate failed* | Description works without the sidecar | ≥16/20 inspection gate passes |
| Local enrichment option (2.1) | Privacy claim true without a caveat | Ollama path works offline |

### Later — reorganise around what we learned

| Initiative | Outcome | Metric |
|---|---|---|
| Unified archive + real facets (3.1, 3.3) | Browsing narrows meaningfully | Facets return useful subsets |
| Search-first home (3.2) | Home matches the job | Retrieval time drops further |
| Revisit `/messages` as a destination | IA matches the corpus | — |
| Decide notes' future | Stop building for a phantom behaviour | Used or removed |

### Risks

- **The retrieval experiment fails and the answer is "reels are not retrievable from metadata."** Then the honest conclusion is that this should be a thumbnail-scanning tool, not a search tool — a real possible outcome, and worth knowing in a week rather than a year.
- **Backfilling 198 items ships three years of private DM content to Groq in one burst.** Do the disclosure first; this is the one ordering constraint in the roadmap.
- **Sync throttles** (20h / 6h cooldown) make ingest-time enrichment slow to validate.
- **Single user, no external validation.** Every assumption in §1 rests on one person's introspection.

## 6. Proposed `PRODUCT.md` replacement

Not yet applied. Replaces the stale purpose and the false ingest principle.

```markdown
## Product Purpose

A private, local archive that makes three years of saved Instagram content
retrievable — reels and posts from the Saved tab, and the reels traded inside
DM threads (in practice, the large majority). It describes everything it
captures on arrival, so the hoard can be searched by what things are about
rather than what they were called. A library, not a feed.

## Strategic Principles

- Retrieval over feed: search and browse are the point; no infinite scroll.
- Nothing enters the archive undescribed. Description is an ingest property,
  not a button the user must find.
- The content is the hero; chrome recedes.
- Private by default: passcode-gated, single user, SQLite on the user's own
  machine. Any content leaving the machine for enrichment is disclosed and
  consented to first.
- Honest about state: the UI always says how stale the vault is and how much
  of it has actually been described.
```

**Changed and why:** purpose now leads with DM content (95% of the corpus); the "official export, not a live sync" principle is deleted (the live sync shipped); description-on-ingest is promoted to a principle (the root cause of every retrieval failure); the privacy principle now admits the enrichment hop; honesty-about-state is added because an 85-day-stale, 0%-described vault currently presents itself as complete.
